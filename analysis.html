2025 Colts Offense: Soft Schedule or Real Decline?¶

Hypothesis: the Colts' hot start was driven by facing easier defenses early, and the offense regressed once the schedule got tougher.

Second question: how much of the late-season collapse comes from Daniel Jones' Week 14 injury instead?

The analysis focuses on early downs (1st and 2nd) and splits the 2025 regular season into three segments:

Segment Weeks Starting QB
Early 1–8 Daniel Jones
Late 9–13 Daniel Jones
Post-injury 14–18 Riley Leonard / Philip Rivers

Run python ingest.py first to build nfl_data.db.

In [1]:
import sqlite3
import pandas as pd
import seaborn as sns
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

conn = sqlite3.connect('nfl_data.db')
sns.set_style('whitegrid')

# Every query uses the same segment definition, so it lives in one place.
# Each segment has an explicit upper bound: nothing after Week 13 can leak into 'Late'.
SEGMENTS = ['Early (1-8)', 'Late (9-13)', 'Post-injury (14-18)']
SEGMENT_SQL = """
    CASE
        WHEN week <= 8 THEN 'Early (1-8)'
        WHEN week <= 13 THEN 'Late (9-13)'
        ELSE 'Post-injury (14-18)'
    END
"""

# 2025 regular-season early-down run/pass plays
EARLY_DOWN_FILTER = """
    season = 2025
    AND season_type = 'REG'
    AND play_type IN ('run', 'pass')
    AND down IN (1, 2)
"""

2. Load Colts early-down plays¶

One query pulls every Colts 1st/2nd down run or pass, tagged with its segment. The sanity check below proves each segment only contains its own weeks.

Plays are classified by what was called, not how they ended. In this data a QB scramble has play_type = 'run' even though the play was called as a pass, so play_call uses the pass (dropback) flag instead: every dropback, including sacks and scrambles, counts as a called pass, and only designed runs count as runs.

In [3]:
query = f"""
SELECT
    week,
    {SEGMENT_SQL} AS segment,
    defteam,
    down,
    ydstogo,
    CASE WHEN pass = 1 THEN 'pass' ELSE 'run' END AS play_call,
    run_location,
    yards_gained,
    epa
FROM pbp_data
WHERE posteam = 'IND'
  AND {EARLY_DOWN_FILTER}
"""
colts = pd.read_sql_query(query, conn)
colts['segment'] = pd.Categorical(colts['segment'], categories=SEGMENTS, ordered=True)
colts['down'] = colts['down'].astype(int)

# Success: gain >= 4 yards on 1st down, or >= half the yards to go on 2nd down
colts['is_success'] = (
    ((colts['down'] == 1) & (colts['yards_gained'] >= 4)) |
    ((colts['down'] == 2) & (colts['yards_gained'] >= colts['ydstogo'] / 2))
).astype(int)

# Sanity check: first and last week in each segment
colts.groupby('segment', observed=True)['week'].agg(first_week='min', last_week='max', plays='count')

3. Testing the hypothesis: how tough were the Colts' opponents?¶

Each defense is rated by the average early-down EPA it allowed over the full 2025 regular season, counting only its games against other teams so the Colts' own results don't affect the rating. Lower EPA allowed = better defense. Rank 1 is the best defense in the league.

A full-season rating is more stable than one built from a handful of games in each segment.

In [4]:
query = f"""
SELECT
    defteam,
    AVG(epa) AS epa_allowed,
    RANK() OVER (ORDER BY AVG(epa)) AS def_rank
FROM pbp_data
WHERE {EARLY_DOWN_FILTER}
  AND posteam != 'IND'
GROUP BY defteam
"""

6. Did play-calling follow what was working?¶

Split early downs into three situations and compare called runs with called passes in each segment. League averages show what's normal: across the NFL, passing usually beats running on 1st down and 2nd-and-long, while 2nd-and-short is close to even.

def situation(df):
    return pd.Series(

How confident can we be that runs really beat passes on 2nd down in Weeks 9–13? A bootstrap resamples the plays 10,000 times and recomputes the run-minus-pass gap each time, giving a 95% range for the true gap. If that range includes 0, the sample is too small to rule out "no real difference."

In [13]:
import numpy as np

rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
late_2nd = colts[(colts['segment'] == 'Late (9-13)') & (colts['down'] == 2)]
run_epa = late_2nd.loc[late_2nd['play_call'] == 'run', 'epa'].to_numpy()
pass_epa = late_2nd.loc[late_2nd['play_call'] == 'pass', 'epa'].to_numpy()

gaps = [rng.choice(run_epa, len(run_epa)).mean() - rng.choice(pass_epa, len(pass_epa)).mean()
        for _ in range(10_000)]
low, high = np.percentile(gaps, [2.5, 97.5])

print(f'Weeks 9-13, 2nd down: {len(run_epa)} runs, {len(pass_epa)} passes')
print(f'Run minus pass EPA: {run_epa.mean() - pass_epa.mean():+.3f}')
print(f'95% range: {low:+.3f} to {high:+.3f}')
print(f'Share of resamples where runs come out ahead: {np.mean(np.array(gaps) > 0):.0%}')

Findings¶

Plays are classified by what was called: every dropback (including sacks and scrambles) is a pass, and only designed runs are runs.

Segment Avg opponent defense rank (1 = best) EPA opponents allow Colts EPA/play (avg of games) Colts vs. expected Success rate
Early (1–8) 16.6 +0.014 +0.202 +0.189 57.8%
Late (9–13) 11.3 −0.046 +0.031 +0.077 56.1%
Post-injury (14–18) 9.6 −0.045 −0.184 −0.140 42.2%

1. The schedule did get tougher. Opponents went from middle-of-the-league defenses (average rank 16.6) in Weeks 1–8 to defenses ranked around 10th on average for the rest of the season.

2. But the hot start wasn't a soft schedule. Early opponents allowed +0.014 EPA per play, almost exactly the league average of +0.016, and the Colts beat what those defenses usually allowed by +0.189. That includes two of the league's best defenses: Denver (#2, +0.420 over expected) and the Chargers (#4, +0.271).

3. Schedule explains part of the Weeks 9–13 decline, not all of it. Tougher defenses account for about 0.06 EPA per play (+0.014 → −0.046 allowed), roughly a third of the 0.17 drop in Colts EPA. After adjusting for opponents, the offense still slipped from +0.189 to +0.077 over expected.

4. On early downs, the collapse lines up with the injury, not the schedule. Weeks 9–13 and 14–18 had equally tough opponents (−0.046 vs −0.045 EPA allowed), yet the Colts went from +0.031 to −0.184 EPA per play once the backups took over.

5. Before the injury, the biggest slide was in the 2nd-down passing game. On 1st down, both play types slipped: passes from +0.296 to +0.104 and designed runs from +0.104 to −0.029. On 2nd down they split apart: pass EPA fell from +0.191 to −0.129 while designed runs improved from +0.206 to +0.306. Inside runs also improved, from +0.059 to +0.201.

6. Play-calling in Weeks 9–13 didn't follow what was working on 2nd down. On 1st down it did.

  • 2nd & short (≤6 yards): runs averaged +0.270 EPA and passes −0.535, yet the pass share rose from 27.9% to 38.1%. This is the clearest evidence.
  • All 2nd downs: runs averaged +0.306 and passes −0.129, while the run/pass ratio fell from 0.72 to 0.53 (more passing). This comparison mixes short and long 2nd downs, so 2nd & short is the cleaner test.
  • 2nd & long (7+): only 2 designed runs in Weeks 9–13, so there's not enough data to judge.
  • 1st down: passing was still the better call (+0.104 vs −0.029 for runs), in line with the league-wide pattern (+0.095 vs −0.069). The small rise in pass share (55.0% → 57.5%) was a reasonable decision.

How strong is Finding 6? A bootstrap of 2nd-down plays in Weeks 9–13 (28 runs, 53 passes) puts the run-minus-pass gap at +0.435 EPA, with runs ahead in 90% of resamples. The 95% range (−0.222 to +1.176) still includes zero, so the evidence is suggestive, not conclusive.

7. Across all downs, most of the decline came before the injury. Colts EPA per play on every run and pass fell from +0.241 in Weeks 1–8 to −0.015 in Weeks 9–13, with Jones still starting, then to −0.119 after the injury. The pre-injury drop holds after the same opponent adjustment (+0.241 → +0.013 over expected). 3rd down was the biggest single factor: +0.256 EPA per play in Weeks 1–8, −0.242 in Weeks 9–13, then back to +0.244 after the injury. So the two views differ: on early downs the worst came after the injury, while on 3rd down it came before.

Caveats. Weeks 9–13 is only four games (187 early-down plays, 49 third-down plays) and Weeks 14–18 is five, so single games move segment averages a lot. In Weeks 9–13, 2nd & short has only 26 runs and 16 passes. Defensive ratings use early-down EPA only.
